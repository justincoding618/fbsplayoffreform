Reducing the Bowls

As the playoff expands, the number of bowl games will decrease. This is because the more teams in a given conference earn at-large bids, the less bowl eligible teams available. The number of bowl games that would be cut depends on the number of bowl games that would be kept. If the bowl games begin reducing, this means that making bowl games as consolation games for playoff participants may not be the best idea. The bowl games that are not part of the playoff would have to be repurposed so that they still have some meaning.

There really is not an ideal solution for how much the remaining non-CFP bowl games decrease by. In the end, it all depends on whether bowl games is worth keeping in the long-run. The money and the organizers driving the bowl games would have to make the decisions themselves. It will come down to how many bowl-eligible slots that would be made available after 32 teams make it into the playoff field.

The Scenario

In the project, the 32 teams have been selected to participate in the playoff (which are all conference champions and at-large teams). This would leave however number of teams to participate in stand-alone bowl games. However, that number can vary depending on how many bowl games are retained. Let's take a look at the most ideal possibilities that can happen.

Only 16 Non-CFP Bowl Games Stay

Let's say only the 16 non-CFP bowl games (in terms of payout and/or strong history mentioned in the Future of Bowl Games page) are kept. This means that about 32 more bowl eligible teams would be able to play in a bowl game. The overall number of teams that would then participate in bowl season would be the following:

The advantage here in this scenario is that the selection of teams into bowl season is reduced significantly. Instead of having over 80 teams participate in bowl season, just less than half of Division I FBS would participate in bowl season.

This also leaves a lot of room for additional bowl-eligible teams in case teams that get offered to play in a bowl decide to opt out. Let's say there are 84 teams that are bowl eligible. If 64 teams are selected to participate in bowl season, there would be 20 extra bowl-eligible teams left over. In case at least one team decides to opt out playing in a bowl game and focus on next season, at least one of the 20 extra bowl-eligible teams would get an offer to play in a bowl game.

Let's say that along with the 16 non-CFP bowl games (in terms of payout and/or strong history), four more non-CFP bowl games are kept. This means about 40 more bowl eligible teams would be able to play in a bowl game. The overall number of teams that would then participate in bowl season would be the following:

This still leaves room for additional bowl-eligible teams in case teams that get offered to play in a bowl decide to opt out. However, the pool of remaining bowl-eligible teams would be less. Let's say there are 84 teams that are bowl eligible. If 72 teams are selected to participate in bowl season, there would only be 12 extra bowl-eligible teams left over. In case at least one team decides to opt out playing in a bowl game and focus on next season, at least one extra bowl-eligible team would get an offer to play in a bowl game.

If we kept around 80 bowl eligible teams, this means that 24 non-CFP bowl games would still be kept. While this would be similar to previous seasons since 2015 where at least 80 teams participated in bowl season, this is where it starts to go into saturated territory.

In addition, there is not much room for additional bowl-eligible teams in case teams that get offered to play in a bowl decide to opt out. Let's say there are 84 teams that are bowl eligible. If 80 teams are selected to participate in bowl season, there would be only four extra bowl-eligible teams left over. In case at least one team decides to opt out playing in a bowl game and focus on next season, at least one of the four extra bowl-eligible teams would get an offer to play in a bowl game. However, there is also a chance teams with losing records can be invited as well in case those extra bowl-eligible teams also decide to opt out or there are no more bowl-eligible teams.

Eliminating All Non-CFP Bowl Games

Let's say that all the non-CFP Bowl games are eliminated. This would be the doomsday scenario where any team that misses the playoffs would end their seasons. The overall number of teams that would then participate in bowl season would be the following:

The teams that miss the FBS playoff, but are bowl-eligible, will not have any postseason opportunity since all of the non-CFP Bowl games would be eliminated. Basically, all of the remaining bowl-eligible teams would end their season.

This would likely be the worst-case scenario for the pool of bowl games. This is because some of the non-CFP bowl games have a strong history and/or have very high payouts to teams participating. In addition, lower NCAA Divisions still hold bowl games for teams that did not make the playoffs or have contractual ties to play in them. In Division I FCS, every conference except for the MEAC and SWAC participate in the playoffs. The MEAC and SWAC conference champions play in the Celebration Bowl for the right to win the HBCU National Championship. In Division II, three bowl games are administered for teams that have missed the 32-team playoff field. Finally, Division III administers 15 bowl games for teams that failed to make the 40-team playoff field. While this doomsday scenario is a longshot, it is also not impossible that it happens.

What if the Playoff Expands Beyond 32 Teams?

If the playoff expands beyond 32 teams, then the number of bowl games would have to be reduced further. For example, let's say the playoff expands to 40 teams. If there is an appetite to keep 80 bowl eligible teams, then only 20 more bowl games would still be kept. If keeping the top 16 non-CFP bowl games (in terms of payout and/or strong history), then about 32 more bowl eligible teams would play in the those bowl games. For either scenario, the reamining bowl games' future would be in doubt.

Conclusion

Reducing the number of bowl games can become a very difficult decision. When reducing the number of bowl games, it is important to find the right balance of the number of bowl-eligible teams after 32 teams are selected to participate in the FBS playoffs. If done correctly, the bowl season would not become too saturated. However, the biggest disadvantage is that by reducing the number of bowl game opportunities, more bowl-eligible teams would have to end their seasons if they are not selected to play in a bowl game unless teams above them decide to opt-out. However, if the situation calls for bowl game reduction, this solution is definitely worthwhile to consider in order to strike the right balance of the number of bowl-eligible teams after 32 teams are selected to participate in the FBS playoffs.
