Preserving the Bowls for Second Round Participants Only

The main solution for bowl preservation is to incorporate the top four Non-New Year's Six Bowl games as consolation bowl games for eliminated second round participants. The reason I want to put it this way is because I want to keep the first round as very high stakes. This would mean that teams eliminated in the first round would have their seasons end. On the other hand, this would make the incentive for teams to have to win in the first round if they want to at least play in a bowl game.

By implementing the 32-team field solution with consolation bowl games for second round participants, we would have 84 teams total that would participate in any of the playoffs. That would have a selection percentage to about 60.9%. After selecting those 32 teams with at least a 0.500 record, we would have about 52 bowl eligible teams left over.

In this scenario, we would still be able to retain a high quality of the top ten non-New Year’s Six Bowl Games (based on revenue distribution). You would still keep the current quality of the bowl eligible teams while also maintaining the quality of the top non-New Year’s Six Bowl Games. However, we would have to the payout for the consolation bowl games for second round participants is a little higher for this scenario. Regardless, you would basically significantly reduce the problem of teams with losing records participating in bowl games (unless more bowl games are added and teams decide to opt out of bowl games). However, you would still also have the chance of players deciding to sit out of the bowl games after being eliminated from the FBS playoffs. If the secondary playoff system is not implemented, then this solution would do just fine.
