Quarterfinal Campus Sites

Having quarterfinal games at campus sites is one of the most discussed aspects for expanding the playoff. Many fans have suggested quarterfinal games to be played at campus sites. Their reasoning is justified. The quarterfinals usually will have marquee matchups. With marquee matchups, home-field advantage becomes key. The first round of the College Football Playoff has demonstrated how home-field environments can become electric with very high postseason stakes. The biggest trade-off for having quarterfinal campus site games is the bowl games. The bowl games would return to only hosting semifinal games on a rotating basis rather than hosting both the quarterfinals and semifinals. In addition, the bowl line-up in the main solution will have to be adjusted to allow campus sites to host the quarterfinals.

The Main Plan

If there is appetite for the quarterfinals to be hosted at campus sites, the main plan is to have eight bowl games rotate to host the semifinals. The bowls that are not semifinal hosts would then take teams that at least made the second round, but eliminated before reaching the semifinals. The eight bowl games would be dubbed as "New Year's Epic Eight".

Wildcard Bowl Games

The Wildcard Bowl Games would now become a required component if we want quarterfinals at campus sites. Since the top 8 bowl games would take the best teams eliminated in either the second round or quarterfinals, the ReliaQuest and Holiday Bowls would now become part of the consolation pool for eliminated first round participants. Those two bowl games would take the top four rated eliminated first round participants and pit them in the bowl games. There will then be six Wildcard Bowls that would take the 12 remaining eliminated first round participants.

Supplementary Playoffs and the Rest

The secondary and the tertiary playoff supplementary solutions will remain the same. They will still take the best teams that were not selected in the FBS playoffs. The bowl line-ups in those solutions would remain intact. In addition, the overall and further breakdowns of this solution would stay the same. 80 Teams would still have a path to the postseason, whether it would be through the FBS Playoffs or any of the supplementary playoff solutions.

Revenue Model

The revenue model would be a bit different. While the revenue earnings for advancing in each round would remain the same, since conference ties come back into play, the consolation bowl game payouts would be a bit different.

Since the consolation bowl games are going to be administered for second round participants and quarterfinalists, FBS Conferences, Notre Dame, and FBS Independents will receive the following payouts of the consolation bowl games:

Bowl Game FBS Conferences and Notre Dame Non-Notre Dame FBS Independents

As you can see, the Holiday and ReliaQuest Bowls have a slightly higher payout than a typical wildcard bowl game because they are both the top bowl games remaining. This means that if a team is eliminated in the first round and is among the top four first round participants, they would get a better payout than participating in a Wildcard Bowl Game.

By making the quarterfinals happen on campus sites, there is a potential to add an additional $50M due to the most prestigious FBS Playoff consolation bowl games now selecting quarterfinalists along with second round participants. In addition, the Holiday Bowl and ReliaQuest Bowls boost the first round pool by having a bit more payout than a typical Wildcard Bowl Game.

Conclusion

Administering quarterfinal games at campus site has the potential to become worth it if done correctly. The most prestigious bowl games would return to their roots as semifinal hosts only. It has the potential to produce even more revenue than the original solution since quarterfinalists would participate in the prestigious consolation bowl games. The alternative solution is definitely worthwhile to consider if the right opportunity arises.
