Quarterfinal Campus Sites

Having quarterfinal games at campus sites is one of the most discussed aspects for expanding the playoff. Many fans have suggested quarterfinal games to be played at campus sites. Their reasoning is justified. The quarterfinals usually will have marquee matchups. With marquee matchups, home-field advantage becomes key. The first round of the College Football Playoff has demonstrated how home-field environments can become electric with very high postseason stakes. The biggest trade-off for having quarterfinal campus site games is the bowl games. The bowl games would return to only hosting semifinal games on a rotating basis rather than hosting both the quarterfinals and semifinals. In addition, the bowl line-up in the main solution will have to be adjusted to allow campus sites to host the quarterfinals.

The Main Plan

The main solution is going to stay the same except the quarterfinals will function a bit differently. The regional winners would advance to the quarterfinals, which would be hosted at campus sites (and the New Year's Six Bowl Games would not become involved). The quarterfinals would become the Super Regionals, where the higher seeded teams host. This would be similar to the Super Regionals in NCAA Baseball and Softball. The bracket mechanics of the main solution would stay the same.

Eight bowl games would rotate to host the semifinals. The bowls that are not semifinal hosts would then take teams that at least made the second round, but were eliminated before reaching the semifinals. The eight bowl games would be dubbed as "New Year's Epic Eight".

First Three Rounds (Campus Sites)

Home-field advantage for the first three rounds is still determined by seeding:

This time, if a team is a top four national seed, they are guaranteed to host three playoff games. A top eight national seed would still get to host two playoff games. However, their chance to host a quarterfinal game would hinge on whether lower seeded/unseeded teams would be able to pull off upsets vs a Top 4 seed. A top 16 national seed is still guaranteed to host one playoff game. However, their chance to host playoff games in later rounds would hinge on whether lower seeded/unseeded teams would be able to pull off upsets vs a Top 8 seed. Unseeded teams would begin their playoff path on the road. Their chance to host playoff games in later rounds would hinge on whether they are able to pull off upsets vs their seeded counterparts and whether their unseeded counterpart would be able to pull off upsets vs their seeded counterparts. In an event where two unseeded teams face each other in any of the later rounds, home-field is determined through the highest final CFP ranking.

Conclusion

Administering quarterfinal games at campus sites has the potential to become worth it if done correctly. The most prestigious bowl games would return to their roots as semifinal hosts only. It has the potential to produce even more revenue than the original solution since quarterfinalists would participate in the prestigious consolation bowl games. The alternative solution is definitely worthwhile to consider if the right opportunity arises.
