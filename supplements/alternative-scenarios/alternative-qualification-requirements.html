Alternative Qualification Requirements

The main qualification requirement for the 32-team field is the following:

  • All overall conference champions receive an automatic bid to the playoffs
  • The rest of the playoff field would be filled through at-large bids

However, what if the playoff field wanted only the best teams competing for the National Championship? What if a customized qualification requirement would work better? The biggest trade-off for considering these alternative qualification scenarios is to give up automatic qualification for all conference champions. While having the best teams play for a National Championship is something some do desire, it comes at a cost of leaving out some conference champions from other conferences to play for a National Championship. However, if the desire is still there, then this page will cover the options.

In order for the alternative qualifications to work, the following should be considered:

  • Make sure it's conducive for both the Power and Non-Power FBS conferences
  • Make sure it's fair for all teams competing for a National Championship

The Big Ten has pushed to expand the playoff to 24 teams. While they initially proposed multiple automatic qualifiers for power conferences and non-power conferences, they eventually settled for the 23+1 format, where the top 23 at-large teams plus one automatic bid to the non-power FBS conference would play for a National Championship. However, there has been a recent talk for a 20-team field, where the top 12 teams would earn a bye into the playoff and would contain four play-in games. However, it was quickly dismissed by the CFP management committee. If the 32-team field decides to emphasize more at-large bids, the best solution that could happen is to use a hybrid of the NAIA model with some tweaks to make it still conducive to the modern collegiate landscape.

The Main Alternative Solution

Prior to expanding to a 20-team field, NAIA conference and division champions had to meet a top-20 ranking requirement to earn an automatic bid. Since expanding to 20 teams, all conference and division champions now receive automatic bids regardless of ranking. Here are a few examples of how their prior qualification model happened:

  • In the 2016 NAIA season, 11 automatic bids went to conference and division champions ranked in the top 20, meaning that one conference champion did not make the field, leaving room for five at-large bids
  • In the 2017 NAIA season, all 12 automatic bids went to conference and division champions ranked in the top 20, which only left room for four at-large bids
  • In the 2018 NAIA season, 11 automatic bids went to conference and division champions ranked in the top 20, meaning that one conference champion did not make the field, leaving room for five at-large bids
  • In the 2019 NAIA season, all 12 automatic bids went to conference and division champions ranked in the top 20, which only left room for four at-large bids
  • In the 2020 NAIA season, the qualification methods were temporarily modified where conference and division champions must be ranked in the Top 25. For this change, all 12 automatic bids went to conference and division champions ranked in the top 25, which only left room for four at-large bids
  • In the 2021 NAIA season, all 11 automatic bids went to conference and division champions ranked in the top 20, which only left room for five at-large bids
  • In the 2022 NAIA season, all 11 automatic bids went to conference and division champions ranked in the top 20, which only left room for five at-large bids

The main alternative solution uses a hybrid NAIA model for automatic bids. The following model would work as follows:

  • Any conference champion ranked in the final regular season CFP Top 25 earns an automatic bid
  • The last two automatic bids would go to the top two unranked conference champions. If there is only one unranked conference champion, one of the two automatic bids would be converted to an at-large bid. If all conference champions are ranked, these two automatic bids would be converted to at-large bids.
  • The rest of the field would be filled with at-large bids

This alternative system can provide a way to keep the meaning of automatic bids while also putting in the best teams to make the playoff field. However, there could be scenarios like 2010, 2012, and 2025 where a power conference could have an unranked conference champion. This would mean that each of those conference champions could be in jeopardy of missing the playoffs if they are not among the top two unranked conference champions. There could also be seasons like 2015 and 2017 where at least one conference champion could be left out of the playoff field despite winning at least 10 games.

Secondary and Tertiary Playoff Fields

If any conference champion does not make the playoff field, they would receive an automatic bid to the College Playoff Crown. The College Playoff Crown would still have one automatic bid per conference to send the best remaining non-conference champion into the field. If one of the teams decide to decline an automatic bid to the College Playoff Crown, the automatic bid would be converted to an at-large bid to the best remaining team available. The Football Bowl Playoffs would remain an exclusive at-large berth for remaining teams not selected into the FBS playoffs nor College Playoff Crown.

Conclusion

An alternative selection method proposed here could be worth considering if giving automatic bids to every conference champion is not worth the hassle. The advantage is the flexibility for at-large bids and a stronger emphasis on rewarding the season's best overall teams. However, a disadvantage that can happen is that power conferences can produce unranked conference champions and non-power conferences can produce a conference champion with at least 10 wins, but potentially not make it into the playoff field. The alternative qualification requirement is definitely worthwhile to consider as a compromise as a way to still provide automatic qualifiers while having flexible room for at-large bids.
