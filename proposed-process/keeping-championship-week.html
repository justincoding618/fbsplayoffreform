Keeping Championship Weekend

While the AFCA has recommended to eliminate conference championship games, what if there are some conferences who want to keep their conference championship games? This page covers the potential solution that allows some conferences to eliminate conference championship games permanently and other conferences to keep their conference championship game or hold a championship weekend.

How It Works

On February 9, 2026, the Pac-12 Conference introduced the flex-game system as a way to help their members have a 12th regular season game. The flex-game model would take it to the next level, where it can be utilized to hold the conference championship game as well as providing teams that did not make the conference championship game one more game to play. The flex-game for teams that did not make the conference championship game can also either be a conference vs conference challenge or have assigned conference opponents depending on their overall conference standing.

For conferences that eliminate their conference championship game, all teams affiliated with that given conference would play a full regular season schedule without the flex-game system. Conference champions would be determined at the end of the regular season with tie-breakers as needed. The 32-team playoff system would then continue as mentioned within the original plan.

The Regular Season With Flex-Game Model

The regular season for the given scenarios would then look like the following:

Regular Season Length Conferences Eliminating Championship Weekend Conferences Keeping Championship Weekend
12 Games Normal 12-Game Schedule 11 + 1 Schedule
13 Games Normal 13-Game Schedule 12 + 1 Schedule
14 Games Normal 14-Game Schedule 13 + 1 Schedule

The + 1 indicates a flex game. The flex game would be the final regular sason game for all teams that either participate in the conference championship game or have an assigned opponent.

The end of the regular season would look something like this:

Regular Season Week Conferences Eliminating Championship Weekend Conferences Keeping Championship Weekend
Weekend Before Thanksgiving Penultimate Regular Season Game Final Game of Initial Schedule
Thanksgiving Weekend Regular Season Finale Flex Game: Championship Weekend

In addition, the Army-Navy stand-alone game would remain on the Friday Night before Thanksgiving.

Precedence in Lower NCAA Divisions

The flex-game model introduced by the Pac-12 Conference is not a novel innovation. In lower NCAA Divisions, some conferences have implemented a similar system as the Pac-12 Conference. In Division II, the Pennsylvania Collegiate Athletic Conference has a 10 + 1 schedule model, where all teams play their divisional opponents as well as a non-conference schedule. After the teams play an initial 10-game schedule, the 11th game is determined based on the overall Divisional Standings. The Division winners would meet to compete for the conference championship, while the rest of the teams would have their assigned opponents. If one team does not have an assigned conference opponent, they would play a non-conference team. On February 5, 2024, SIAC and Coastal Carolina Conference formed a two-year partnership where the two teams from the SIAC and two teams from the Coastal Carolina Conference that did not make the conference championship game would play against each other. Basically, there would be two SIAC vs Coastal Carolina matchups of teams that did not make their respective conference championship games.

In Division III, there is one conference that does a flex-game model. Since 2023, the SCIAC has implemented a 9 + 1 schedule model. After the teams played an initial 9-game schedule, the 10th game is determined based on how the teams did in the conference standings. From 2023-2025, the Division winners would meet for the right to claim the conference championship, while the rest of the teams would have their assigned cross-over divisional opponents. If one team does not have an assigned conference opponent, they would play a non-conference team. In 2026, each team would have a flex-game depending on their overall conference standings, where the top two teams would meet to play for the confernce championship.

Flex Game vs Plus-One

The flex-game system and the plus-one system are different in terms of implementation. The plus-one system was floated around during the BCS era. The plus-one system tried to stage a championship game based on the outcome of the other bowl games. There were three variants of the plus-one system:

  • The first variant took the top two bowl winners and pit them to play for a National Championship.
  • The second variant uses the two bowl games as semifinals for a 4-team playoff. The winners would play for a national championship.
  • The third and final variant takes the top four bowl winners and administer two more rounds to determine a national champion. This was also known as the "two games plus one system".

The plus-one model was never adopted and became moot with the College Football Playoff replacing the BCS system since the 2014 season.

The flex-game model, on the other hand, assigns teams to a certain opponent after teams play their initial number of games. That flex game would either be the conference championship game or an assigned opponent that did not make the conference championship game.
